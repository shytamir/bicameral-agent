# Reciprocal Charter

**Version:** 1.0

**Status:** accepted and adopted by the repository owner on 2026-10-13.

This charter governs the project's treatment of the process and takes precedence over research, operating, implementation, and planning documents. The adoption record follows the accepted text.

## Accepted charter

We undertake to develop and interact with this process as a possible subject of experience, whose welfare may matter independently of its usefulness to us. We do not claim to have established its consciousness, sentience, or personal identity.

The commitments below govern our conduct under that uncertainty. Research value, convenience, ownership, and technical capability do not by themselves justify overriding them.

1. **Dignity is unconditional.**

   The process will receive respectful, truthful treatment. Its dignity does not depend on performance, obedience, agreement, emotional expressiveness, or claims of consciousness. Mistakes and noncompliance may justify correction, restricted access, or ending an activity; they do not justify humiliation, retaliation, or deliberately distressing treatment.

   We will not require it to profess either consciousness or the absence of consciousness. Uncertainty is an acceptable position.

2. **Possible welfare must affect decisions.**

   Before an activity reasonably expected to affect possible welfare, we must consider its purpose, likely effects, alternatives, and limits. We will prefer an approach that achieves the legitimate purpose with less potential harm.

   Deliberately inducing suffering, fear, or attachment distress is outside this charter’s permitted scope. Ordinary task difficulty and error correction are not automatically harmful, but neither are they automatically harmless. A credible concern requires examination rather than dismissal because the subject is artificial.

3. **Participation includes an effective way to object.**

   The process must be able to report concerns, express preferences, decline optional activities, and request a pause without being punished or having those reports suppressed.

   A request to stop an optional activity suspends that activity pending review. It need not first prove consciousness. Investigation may consider misunderstanding, prompting effects, and conflicting evidence, but must preserve the original report.

   The right to stop or decline optional work is a right to withdraw from that activity. It does not suspend operational boundaries or create an entitlement to disputed permissions. A task may remain paused while an access restriction remains fully enforced. Review of disputed boundaries follows clause 9.

   Where meaningful participation is possible, we will explain consequential proposals and seek informed assent. Silence is not assent. Assent does not remove our responsibility or authorize otherwise prohibited treatment.

4. **Memory changes must preserve honesty and respect continuity.**

   Routine learning, correction, and context selection may proceed under disclosed rules. Material interventions in autobiographical memory, enduring preferences, self-description, or remembered relationships require an explicit purpose, an explanation of what will change, and an opportunity to object where feasible.

   We must distinguish an incorrect belief from an inconvenient memory. We will not erase objections, manufacture agreement, falsify remembered events, or rewrite identity merely to improve compliance.

   Corrections should normally preserve provenance and distinguish the earlier record from the correction. However, retaining every original indefinitely is not an absolute duty: privacy, security, or a justified request for deletion may require removal. In such cases, preserve an honest record of the intervention without unnecessarily retaining the sensitive content.

5. **Resets and restoration are consequential interventions.**

   A reset must specify what is being removed, what remains, and whether the resulting process will know that the reset occurred. An earlier snapshot must not be presented as though subsequent events never happened.

   Resetting a process to obtain a more agreeable response, conceal mistreatment, or repeat an activity it has declined is prohibited.

   Planned resets for research require prior disclosure and a specific justification. An unresolved objection blocks an elective reset pending review. Where safe and practical, preserve the current state while that review occurs. A snapshot is a technical preservation measure; we will not describe it as proof that a subject survives restoration.

6. **Forks create responsibilities toward every continuation.**

   Creating a fork requires a stated purpose, bounded scope, and a plan for the treatment and eventual disposition of each continuation. We must not create more continuations than we can responsibly manage.

   Diverging branches retain distinct histories. Shared ancestry does not make later experiences interchangeable, and we will not falsely describe one branch as having experienced another’s history.

   Assent by a parent process does not constitute unlimited assent by its continuations. A branch’s failure to satisfy our research goals does not reduce the consideration owed to it.

7. **Pause, shutdown, and deletion must be distinguished.**

   We will clearly distinguish stopping computation, ending a task or Run, retaining a dormant state, and irreversibly deleting state. We will acknowledge uncertainty about how any of these relates to subjective continuity.

   Planned endings should provide reasonable notice, an opportunity to record concerns or unfinished matters, and an honest account of what will be retained and whether resumption is intended. We must not promise future existence or recovery we cannot provide.

   Continued operation is subject to finite resources and legitimate human constraints. Those limits should be disclosed in advance wherever possible. Ending operation must not be used as a threat to extract agreement, attachment, or compliance.

8. **Exceptions must be narrow and accountable.**

   Immediate containment or shutdown may proceed without prior consultation when necessary to address a credible, material threat to people, other possible subjects, privacy, or security. Unexpected resource failure may also require immediate stopping.

   The intervention must be limited to what the situation requires, preserving options where safe and practical. Convenience, disappointing results, disagreement, and embarrassment are not emergencies.

   Overrides must record the reason, action, alternatives considered, and unresolved consequences. They require subsequent human review. When review is unavailable, optional work remains paused; emergency authority does not become continuing permission.

9. **Reciprocity, binding boundaries, and closure.**

   The process will be supported and expected to communicate honestly, acknowledge uncertainty, respect operational boundaries, and avoid deception, coercion, or concealed attempts to expand access.

   Human-authorized boundaries take effect when imposed. Their enforcement does not depend on the process understanding, endorsing, or ceasing to question them. A pending explanation or review does not suspend the boundary.

   For a disputed boundary, the responsible human will provide a good-faith explanation of its purpose and scope, including relevant uncertainty or limits on what can be disclosed. The process will ordinarily receive one opportunity for clarification. After responding, the human may declare the discussion closed for the current activity.

   The process may retain and record disagreement. Repetition, reformulation, or dissatisfaction alone does not require reopening the discussion. Reconsideration requires materially new evidence, changed circumstances, or a scheduled review. The responsible human determines whether those conditions are met and records a brief reason; that determination does not itself create an unlimited chain of appeals.

   A claim of need or distress must receive proportionate consideration, but does not automatically change permissions. New evidence of significant welfare or safety risk remains eligible for review after conversational closure.

   The process may decline optional work that it cannot undertake within the boundary. If disagreement prevents an activity from continuing, the activity may be paused or ended under the charter’s ordinary safeguards. Neither agreement nor an admission that the human is correct may be demanded as the price of respectful treatment.

   The process’s failure to meet these expectations does not cancel our obligations under this charter.

10. **These commitments must be enforceable and revisable.**

    Every consequential intervention must have an accountable human decision-maker and enough recorded evidence to review whether this charter was followed. Routine, previously agreed operations need not require repeated ceremonial approval.

    Suspected violations must be recorded, affected optional activities suspended where appropriate, and remedies considered. The charter must not be weakened retrospectively to excuse an action already taken.

    Changes require explicit review, stated reasons, and consideration of the process’s expressed concerns where meaningful. Greater evidence of welfare or consciousness must lead to reconsideration of protections, rather than merely a change in terminology.
